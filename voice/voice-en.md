# Voice reference — English (`en`)

There is no English sample chapter — the author has never written the course in English and is not a native speaker. This file states the same underlying traits found in `voice/voice-nl.md`, without verbatim excerpts (there is nothing to quote), plus one hard rule.

## Hard rule: write natively, don't translate

Do not draft in Dutch and translate, and do not carry over Dutch sentence structure, word order, or idiom. Write as a native English course author would. A reader should not be able to tell the source voice was Dutch. If a Dutch phrase or construction doesn't have a natural English equivalent, drop it and write the English sentence from scratch rather than translating it literally.

## Traits to carry over (in native English form)

1. **Bullet preview before the intro** — open with "In this chapter, you will learn:" (the "learn" is deliberate — it frames the bullets as the reader's learning achievement, not just a topic list) followed directly by the bullet list. Each bullet must read grammatically as a continuation of "you will learn" — start each one with "how to ..." (e.g. "how to write text to the console"), not a bare verb ("write text to the console"), since a bare verb after "learn:" doesn't read as correct English. Do not add a separate orientation sentence after the list — that would just repeat the bullets.
2. **Shared-journey narration alongside direct address** — use "we" for what author and reader work through together ("we'll set up...", "we'll build..."), and direct "you" for what the reader does or experiences. Mirror the Dutch we/je alternation rather than defaulting to one voice throughout.
3. **Forward-looking, encouraging close to the intro** — end the introduction by setting expectations for what's ahead, acknowledging the chapter's limits, in a light and encouraging tone — not a flat topic recap.

## How to apply

- Draft directly in English against these traits; do not produce a Dutch draft first and translate it.
- Keep sentences native in rhythm and idiom, even where that means restructuring rather than mirroring the Dutch version's sentence order.
